ENERGY

Crude Oil (WTI)

+0.52%
September WTI crude oil (CLU26) closed up +0.44 (+0.52%) on Tuesday, after earlier being up as much as +0.80 (+0.95%) and reaching a 3-week high.

Key Drivers

Reverse Factors

  • Any de-escalation in the Ukraine conflict could remove the war premium supporting wheat prices.

Key Themes

  • Energy markets were led higher by geopolitical supply concerns: crude and gasoline climbed as the Strait of Hormuz remained closed, while natural gas jumped on hot US temperature forecasts.
  • Ag markets were mixed: corn and cotton faded from early support to close mixed/steady, soybeans held gains on lower crop-tour pod counts, and wheat saw spring wheat strength but winter wheat weakness.
  • Soft commodities were split: sugar and coffee rallied on supply concerns, while cocoa fell on favorable West African growing conditions.
  • Livestock had a mixed/positive tone: live cattle closed mostly higher on a tight supply narrative despite near-term feedlot pressure, while hog futures fell even as cash prices firmed.
  • In macro, stocks slid and boosted dollar liquidity demand, pushing the dollar index higher. The broader commodity complex continued to see solid fund buying, though a few markets showed turnaround activity.

Risks Highlighted

  • Geopolitical supply support for crude/gasoline could unwind quickly if the Strait of Hormuz reopens or Middle East tensions ease.
  • Weather-driven moves in natural gas and cocoa are vulnerable to forecast changes.
  • Fading early gains in corn, soybeans, and cotton suggests the market may struggle to sustain rallies; profit-taking risk remains.
  • The Ukraine war premium in wheat could reverse if the conflict de-escalates.
  • Larger near-term feedlot inventories could pressure cattle despite the longer-term tight supply picture.
  • The crack spread (CRAK) is flagged as short-term overbought, raising pullback risk in the energy complex.
