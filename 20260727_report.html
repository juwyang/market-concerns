Key Themes

  • **Broad commodity sell-off:** A sharp decline in crude oil (down ~7.5%) led losses across grains, livestock, and softs, with the exception of coffee and cotton.
  • **Weather-driven pressures:** Improving weather forecasts for the U.S. corn and soybean belt pressured grain prices, reversing earlier heat-driven gains.
  • **Livestock under pressure:** Cattle futures slumped on border reopening news and data showing the U.S. herd has stopped shrinking; hogs fell on pork stock builds.
  • **Mixed softs:** Coffee gained on Brazil harvest delays, while cocoa fell on abundant supplies and sugar declined on crude-linked weakness.
  • **Dollar steady:** The dollar edged up on strong capital goods orders, but gains were contained by easing geopolitical tensions.

Risks Highlighted

  • **US-Iran diplomacy outcome:** Crude oil’s drop was driven by hopes for a deal; failure could reverse the decline and impact all commodities.
  • **Weather forecast shifts:** The improving outlook for U.S. grains could quickly change back to hot/dry conditions, reigniting supply fears.
  • **Border reopening impact:** Cattle prices may face further weakness if increased imports materialize, but could also be oversold.
  • **Coffee harvest delay:** While supporting prices now, any acceleration of the Brazilian harvest could cause a sharp reversal.
  • **Cocoa oversupply persistence:** Abundant global supplies remain a structural headwind for cocoa prices.

Watch Next

  • **Weekly USDA Crop Progress ratings** – noted to have fallen after the close on Monday (Article 6), which may support corn and soybeans.
  • **Bloom Energy Q2 earnings report** – scheduled for July 28 (Article 23), could influence energy-related stocks.
  • **Further crude oil volatility** – as US-Iran diplomacy evolves, oil price moves will likely continue to affect grains, sugar, and the dollar.
  • **US economic data** – next readings on durable goods or capital goods could impact the dollar and broader market sentiment.
