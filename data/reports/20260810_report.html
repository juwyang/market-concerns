Key Themes

  • Energy markets were dominated by Middle East supply fears: crude and gasoline both rallied more than 5%, while natural gas rose on hot US weather forecasts.
  • Grains were mixed to modestly firm: soybeans gained on a China sale and slipping ratings, corn was nearly unchanged, wheat closed mixed, and soymeal was lower.
  • Livestock firmed, with live cattle supported by cash trade and no deliveries, while lean hog futures rose despite a lower cash hog price.
  • Soft commodities were mixed: cocoa and sugar gained, arabica coffee fell, cotton slipped, and robusta coffee edged higher.
  • The US dollar firmed on higher T-note yields and crude strength, while utility/power stocks remained in focus on AI-driven electricity demand.

Risks Highlighted

  • Middle East tensions, particularly doubts about reopening the Strait of Hormuz, keep global oil supply at risk.
  • Hot US weather forecasts and global weather risks could disrupt demand and supply dynamics for natural gas and soft commodities.
  • Smaller West African cocoa production threatens supply expectations.
  • US soybean export inspections are down year over year, and Chinese diplomatic criticism describes “tortuous” US trade policy as hurting American farmers.
  • A firmer dollar and higher Treasury yields could pressure commodity prices.
  • Livestock production levels lag, with cash hog prices falling and chart momentum fading for cattle and hogs.
