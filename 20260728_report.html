Daily Financial Briefing

Date: 2026-07-28

ENERGY

Crude Oil

-4.06%
Declined sharply. September WTI crude closed down -3.35 (-4.06%), and September RBOB gasoline closed down -0.0253 (-0.80%). Midday reports also showed crude down $3.25-$3.48 per barrel.

Key Drivers

  • Renewed hopes of a US-Iran peace deal, which could increase global crude supply.

Reverse Factors

  • If peace negotiations fail or stall, crude prices could rebound. The US dollar index also fell, which typically supports crude, but this was outweighed by supply optimism.
Short-term influencer - DOWN

Natural Gas

-3.79%
Fell for a fourth straight session. August Nymex natural gas closed down -0.105 (-3.79%), reaching a 3-month nearest-futures low.

Key Drivers

  • Cooler US weather forecasts reducing demand expectations.

Reverse Factors

  • Weather forecasts could change to hotter conditions, or supply disruptions might tighten the market.
Short-term influencer - DOWN

GRAINS

Corn

Closed higher, recovering Monday's weakness. Front-month contracts rose 5 to 6 ¾ cents; midday gains of 7 to 8 cents were reported. Early trade was fractionally higher.

Key Drivers

  • Deteriorating crop conditions (ratings slip) giving bulls a reason to buy, creating a "Turnaround Tuesday" bounce.

Reverse Factors

  • If weather improves or crop ratings stabilize, the rally could fade. Additionally, ongoing weakness in crude oil may weigh on overall commodity sentiment.
Short-term influencer - UP

Soybeans

Modest recovery after sharp Monday losses. Contracts closed up 2 ½ to 6 ¼ cents; midday saw gains of 7 to 9 ½ cents. Morning trade had been slightly lower.

Key Drivers

  • Bounce following Monday's decline, supported by slippage in crop ratings.

Reverse Factors

  • Monday's weakness was partly due to an improving weather forecast; if that outlook persists, selling pressure could return.
Short-term influencer - UP

Wheat

Mixed trade. Chicago SRW front months fractionally to 2 ½ cents higher, while deferreds were steady to 3 ¾ cents lower. KC HRW futures were down 2 to 3 cents at midday.

Key Drivers

  • The wheat complex faced weakness overall, with CBT trying to hold up. Rumors regarding Russia-Ukraine weighed on prices (article 24 suggests selling opportunity on price weakness).

Reverse Factors

  • If Russia-Ukraine tensions escalate or supply disruptions occur, prices could reverse higher.
Short-term influencer - DOWN

LIVESTOCK

Live Cattle

Bounced back on Tuesday. Futures closed up $1.22 to $3.80 across most contracts, after falling $1.85 to $8.22 on Monday. Midday gains were $1.97 to $3.30.

Key Drivers

  • Technical bounce from Monday’s steep losses. Early bids of $228 were reported, slightly below last week’s $230-231 trade.

Reverse Factors

  • If cash trade remains slow or bids weaken further, the bounce may be short-lived.
Short-term influencer - UP

Lean Hogs

Rose on Tuesday. Futures closed up 12 cents to $1.45; midday trade was steady to 75 cents higher.

Key Drivers

  • Higher USDA national base hog price (up 31 cents to $101.00) and a rising CME Lean Hog Index.

Reverse Factors

  • Pork demand could soften, or processor margins may tighten, reversing gains.
Short-term influencer - UP

SOFTS

Cotton

Closed with losses of 3 to 45 points, despite midday gains of 7 to 15 points. Morning trade had been down 54 to 92 points.

Key Drivers

  • Weakness in crude oil prices and a quiet US dollar index decline that failed to support. Weekly crop progress reports were also a factor.

Reverse Factors

  • The US dollar index fell, which could normally boost dollar-denominated cotton; a crude oil rebound might also lift prices.
Short-term influencer - DOWN

Sugar

-0.21%
Fell to 1-week lows. October NY world sugar #11 closed down -0.03 (-0.21%), and London white sugar closed down -1.60 (-0.35%).

Key Drivers

  • Sinking crude oil prices weighed on sugar, as lower oil reduces demand for ethanol (a sugar coproduct).

Reverse Factors

  • The US dollar index also weakened, which usually supports sugar; if crude stabilizes, sugar could recover.
Short-term influencer - DOWN

Cocoa

+1.98%
Recovered sharply from 3.5-week lows. September NY cocoa closed up +101 (+1.98%), and London cocoa up +69 (+1.80%).

Key Drivers

  • Dollar weakness spurred short-covering, lifting prices.

Reverse Factors

  • If the dollar strengthens again, cocoa could come under renewed selling pressure.
Short-term influencer - UP

Coffee

+4.58%
Surged for a second day, reaching 2-week highs. September arabica closed up +14.85 (+4.58%), and robusta up +78 (+2.05%).

Key Drivers

  • Tightness in the near-term supply outlook and global supply fears.

Reverse Factors

  • If supply fears ease (e.g., improved crop forecasts or export flows), prices could retreat.
Short-term influencer - UP

Key Themes

  • **Crude oil’s sharp decline** due to renewed US-Iran peace deal hopes dragged down energy and some soft commodities (cotton, sugar).
  • **Grains and livestock saw a “Turnaround Tuesday”** recovery after Monday’s losses, supported by deteriorating crop conditions and technical buying.
  • **Dollar weakness** from unexpected consumer confidence drop spurred short-covering in cocoa and contributed to mixed commodity movements.
  • **Coffee surged** on persistent supply fears, while **cocoa rebounded** on dollar-induced short covering.
  • **Tesla’s negative earnings news** highlighted concerns about its growth strategy.

Risks Highlighted

  • Failure of US-Iran peace negotiations could reverse crude oil’s sharp decline.
  • Weather forecasts for the US (cooler temps for nat‑gas, improved crop conditions for grains) could shift and cause price reversals.
  • Further weakening of US consumer confidence could pressure the dollar and risk‑on sentiment.
  • Renewed strength in the dollar would likely weigh on cocoa and other dollar‑denominated commodities.

Watch Next
